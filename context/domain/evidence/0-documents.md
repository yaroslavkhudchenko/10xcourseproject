## Product goals

- Problem: "A shopper who buys the same drugstore products again and again does not know, at the moment of buying, which of their usual shops has the lowest current price for that exact product. Today they check two or three shop sites by hand before each purchase, and they repeat that check every time the product runs out." (`context/foundation/prd.md:22`)
- Value proposition: "What is missing is a view focused on the exact product and the exact shops the shopper buys from." (`context/foundation/prd.md:24`)
- Scale: "the intended scale is a handful of people on one private deployment, with fetches made on demand and bounded by the per-shop request cap." (`context/foundation/prd.md:24`)
- Target user: "the product owner, a shopper who buys the same drugstore products repeatedly across several chains and currently checks two or three shop sites by hand before buying. They reach for the product at the moment they are about to buy one of those items and want to know which shop to buy it from." (`context/foundation/prd.md:28`)
- Primary success criteria: "A signed-in user adds a repeat-purchase product by name and size, confirms the matching item in each supported shop once, and from then on sees for that product which shop is cheapest today, with regular and promo price, the Omnibus 30-day low and the age of each price." and "Prices refresh when the user opens a product or asks for a refresh (a daily automatic refresh is planned after the MVP), and once a product has history the comparison says whether today's price is a good one." (`context/foundation/prd.md:38-39`)
- Secondary success criteria: "For most repeat purchases the owner actually buys at the shop the app names, instead of checking sites by hand." (`context/foundation/prd.md:43`); "Fetching covers the supported shops well enough that manual price entry (FR-009, nice-to-have) is never missed." (`context/foundation/prd.md:44`)
- Guardrails: "Stale or failed prices are visible, never silent: when a fetch fails or a shop changes its response, the user sees the age of the price or a clear gap, never a wrong number presented as current." (`context/foundation/prd.md:49`); "Watchlists stay private: no user can see or infer another user's watchlist; only price observations are shared." (`context/foundation/prd.md:50`); "Online prices are labelled as online prices and never presented as the shelf price in a physical store." (`context/foundation/prd.md:51`)
- Business logic: "For a product bought repeatedly, the app names which of the shopper's shops is cheapest right now and says whether that price is a good one against the product's own price history, or against the shop's declared 30-day low until that history exists." (`context/foundation/prd.md:198`)
- Non-goals: "No open sign-up: the deployment stays private and invite-only." (`context/foundation/prd.md:220`); "No shared household watchlists: one watchlist per person." (`context/foundation/prd.md:221`); "No shelf prices and no store-level data: online prices only, labelled as such." (`context/foundation/prd.md:222`)

## Change goals

Each change folder's `change.md` states that change's intent, and its `plan.md` its desired end state: for example the most recent, "Let the user add a product that Rossmann doesn't sell, found through the other shops' search (Natura, Hebe, Super-Pharm), and compare it like any other watched product." (`context/changes/add-from-other-shops/change.md:12`). These are not used to judge the Core.

## Limitations

- The PRD was drafted before the repository existed; its earlier drafting history is not in git. Its functional requirements were amended by dated update notes rather than rewritten, so a requirement's current form is its last update.
- `README.md` is mostly the starter template's text and says little about the product.
- `CLAUDE.md` describes the product as implemented; it is a reference written alongside the code, not an independent statement of the business.
